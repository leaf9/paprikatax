## 1. The strategic thesis in one paragraph

PaprikaTax is selling a **professional risk-transfer service** — a licensed CPA putting his signature
on a defensible wage so a family can move income to their children without losing it in an audit — but
it is **priced, packaged, and marketed as a $97 information product**. That mismatch is the root of the
commercial problem. It makes the offer non-credible to the buyers who would pay most, impossible to
acquire profitably on paid media, and dependent on a single low-margin transaction with no follow-on
revenue. The fix is not louder ads. It is: **split the jobs currently crushed into the $97 price point**
(lead magnet vs. profit center vs. relationship), **re-price to match the nature of the work**,
**feed the funnel with the genuinely cheap attention the story creative already earns**, and
**concentrate the consumer push on Q4, when the strategy has a real deadline.** In parallel, build the
advisor channel, which has structurally better economics than consumer acquisition ever will.

## 2. Market viability: is there a business here?

**Verdict: yes, at small-business scale — but it is a narrow, education-dependent market that rewards
intent and partner channels far more than interruption advertising.**

### Sizing from the top down

| Layer | Count | Source / logic |
|---|---|---|
| US small businesses | **36.2M** | SBA 2025 profiles |
| — Nonemployer firms (no payroll) | 29.8M | 86.7% are sole proprietorships |
| — Employer firms | 6.3M | 55% S-corps; 12.3% sole props |

The 29.8M nonemployer figure is **mostly a mirage for this product**: nonemployer establishments
average roughly **$59k of annual revenue** ($1.8T across 30.4M establishments). A business at that
level has neither the profit to pay a child a meaningful wage nor a marginal tax rate high enough to
make the arbitrage worth $97, let alone the effort.

The binding constraint is **owner income**, not business count. Applying the three real filters:

| Filter | Estimate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Business owners with ~$150k+ owner income | ~5M | ~40% of employer firms + ~8–10% of nonemployers |
| × has a child aged 7–22 | ~30% | Business owners skew 35–64 |
| **Serviceable market** | **~1.5M households** | |
| **High-value sweet spot** | **~400–600k** | Sole prop / single-member LLC, kids 10–17 (see §3) |

At **0.5% lifetime penetration of the serviceable market** and a $500 blended lifetime value, that is
~7,500 customers and ~$3.75M. **This is a viable specialty practice, not a venture-scale business** —
which is the correct ambition for a CPA firm.

### Why demand exists but conversion has been hard

Demand is real and rising: "hire your kids" is actively promoted by finance podcasts and tax content
creators, which means a meaningful share of the market **arrives pre-sold on the concept** and is
searching for *how*, not *whether*. That is the single most important market fact in this document,
because it tells us where the buyers are: **in search, and in the "already doing it badly" segment** —
not in a cold Facebook feed being introduced to the idea for the first time.

### What makes it hard (structural, must be designed around)

1. **Compound qualification.** Owns a business **and** has working-age kids **and** earns enough
   **and** isn't already doing it. Each "and" multiplies waste in broad-reach channels.
2. **Considered, effort-bearing purchase.** Requires trust in an unknown firm plus real work after
   purchase (intake, then actually running payroll). Not an impulse buy at any price.
3. **Entity type changes the value by 2×** (see §3). The same message is worth very different amounts
   to a sole prop vs. an S-corp owner.
4. **Deferred, probabilistic benefit** — "estimates," "may not save you money." Weak urgency for
   10 months of the year.

## 3. Ideal customer profile

### The qualification math that defines the ICP

The value of this strategy is driven by **entity type**, and it is not a small difference:

| Owner situation | What the child's wage saves | Relative value |
|---|---|---|
| **Sole prop / single-member LLC, child under 18** | Federal + state income tax at parent's marginal rate **plus self-employment tax** (wages to an under-18 child of a sole prop are FICA-exempt; under 21 FUTA-exempt) | **Highest** |
| Parent-only partnership, child under 18 | Same relief as above | Highest |
| S-corp / C-corp / multi-member LLC | Income tax arbitrage only — FICA and FUTA apply as with any employee | Roughly half |
| Child 18–22 (any entity) | Income tax arbitrage; FICA applies | Moderate, but larger wages justified |

**Worked example — the sweet spot.** Sole proprietor, ~$250k business income (32% federal + state),
one 15-year-old paid $15,000 for legitimate marketing work:
- Income tax saved at marginal rate: **~$5,500**
- Self-employment tax avoided on the shifted amount: **material additional saving**
- Child's federal tax: **~$0** (under the standard deduction)
- **Annual family benefit: comfortably five figures with two children.**

Against that, **$97 is not a bargain — it is a credibility problem.** A buyer who believes the savings
number cannot believe the price. (See §5.)

### Priority segments

**Segment A — "The Sloppy Doer" · HIGHEST PRIORITY**
Already paying their kids, with no job description, no timesheets, no W-2, money moving from a personal
account. Heard it on a podcast and acted.
- **Why first:** zero concept education needed, already spending money, and carries *latent fear* —
  loss aversion converts faster than gain-seeking. The product is pure risk removal for them.
- **Message:** "You're already paying your kids. Could you defend it?"
- **Where:** search ("is paying my kids legal," "kids on payroll audit"), retargeting, advisor referrals
- **Willingness to pay:** high — they are buying protection for money already at risk

**Segment B — "The Qualified Sole Prop" · PRIMARY CONSUMER TARGET**
Sole prop or single-member LLC, $150–400k owner income, children 10–17, tax-aware but DIY-inclined.
- **Why:** the maximum-savings case; the strategy pays for itself many times over
- **Message:** the family-tax leak (proven hook) + "the defensible maximum, not the timid minimum"
- **Meta targeting (concrete):** age **30–54**, intersect *Parents with preteens (08–12)* /
  *Parents with teenagers (13–17)* with small-business-owner behaviors and tooling interests
  (QuickBooks, Gusto, Shopify, Etsy sellers, entrepreneur media). This intersection is one Meta can
  actually deliver — unlike the current broad "business owner parents."
- **Product fit:** $297 kit; upgrade path to done-with-you

**Segment C — "The Delegator" · HIGHEST VALUE PER SALE**
$400k+ income, S-corp or multiple entities, has a CPA who doesn't do proactive planning. Wants it
handled, not explained.
- **Message:** "Your CPA files. We plan." Done-with-you, $997.
- **Where:** search, LinkedIn, referral, advisor channel
- **Note:** $97 actively repels this buyer. Wrong product, right prospect.

**Segment D — "The College Funder"**
Children 18–22, tuition in progress. Reframe: fund college with pre-tax dollars; the 22-year-old
operations-manager example ($46,229 over time) already speaks to them.
- **Seasonality:** Aug–Sep and Dec–Jan (tuition invoices land)

**Segment E — Tax & financial advisors · CHANNEL, NOT CUSTOMER**
CPAs, EAs, CFPs with business-owner books. One advisor can carry 5–40 qualifying clients.
- Treat as **distribution**, measured on activated advisors and downstream client volume — never on
  cost per lead. (Today it is 59% of spend with no downstream measurement at all.)

### Explicit exclusions

Age 55+ (no working-age dependents), W-2 employees without a business, businesses under ~$75k profit
(the strategy cannot pay), and — once a list exists — existing customers.

## 4. Pain points → message map

Ranked by conversion power, not by how often the client mentions them:

| Rank | Pain (in the buyer's words) | Message | Best segment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "I'm already paying my kids and I have a feeling I'm doing it wrong." | "A beard with no token." Audit exposure is the problem; a signed opinion is the fix. | A |
| 2 | "I'm funding my kids with money the IRS already taxed." | The family-tax leak. Allowance → phone → car → college, all after-tax. | B, D |
| 3 | "My CPA never brings me anything proactive." | "Your CPA files your taxes. Nobody's planning them." | B, C |

**Loss aversion outperforms gain-seeking in tax marketing.** The current creative leads almost entirely
on gain ("save thousands"). At least half the creative slate should lead on exposure and protection —
that is also the honest differentiator against the do-it-yourself crowd.

## 5. Positioning and differentiation

### The competitive frame

| Alternative | What they give | What they don't |
|---|---|---|
| Podcast / TikTok tax content | The idea, free, at enormous reach | Any accountability; no signature; no defense |
| ChatGPT / calculators / blogs | A number | Judgment, and anyone standing behind it |
| The buyer's own CPA | Filing, compliance | Proactive planning; nobody researches wages for a 12-year-old |
| Boutique tax-strategy firms | Full engagement, $3,000–10,000 | Accessible price; speed |
| **PaprikaTax** | **A signed, defensible wage strategy from a licensed CPA firm, in days, for a fraction of an engagement** | | 

**The wedge is genuinely good and genuinely defensible:** the only *accountable* version of a strategy
that is otherwise available only as free advice you can't rely on, or as a five-figure engagement.

### The authority asset that is being wasted

Dave Nagy **founded ReasonableCompensation.com** — software he built for other CPAs to prepare
defensible salary opinions, operated 2011–2015 — and he built it *because an IRS audit of an S-corp
client's reasonable salary left him without a good way to defend it.* He was featured in the
**Journal of Accountancy (May 2012)**.

That is not a generic credential. It is the **exact same problem shape** as this product:
*prove to the IRS that a wage is reasonable.* The positioning line writes itself:

> "For fifteen years I've solved one problem: proving to the IRS that a wage is reasonable.
> First for S-corp owners. Now for your kids."

Today the site reduces this to a bullet. It should be the spine of the trust argument, and it is
credible with third-party citation — the strongest possible answer to "why should I believe you?"

> ⚠️ **Substantiation:** ReasonableCompensation.com operated **2011–2015**. Current site copy
> ("Creator of ReasonableCompensation, **used by accountants nationwide**") reads as present tense and
> should be corrected to past tense. Note also that **RCReports** is a separate, still-operating
> company in this category — do not imply any relationship. Cite the Journal of Accountancy feature
> instead; it is verifiable and does the same work.

### The price-as-signal problem

For a **risk-mitigation product delivered by a licensed professional, $97 reads as implausible.**
The buyer's internal objection is not "too expensive," it is *"how can a real CPA opinion cost $97 —
what am I actually getting?"* Low price undermines the exact authority the product sells.

Compounding it, **$97 cannot carry paid acquisition.** Even a well-built funnel lands at $40–80 CAC;
after processing fees and any support cost, there is no margin to reinvest, and Meta needs ~50
conversions/week to optimize — volume $97 economics can't fund.

**Recommendation: test $297 as the primary price** (against $97 and $497). Hypothesis: unit volume
falls less than price rises, *and* credibility improves — a common outcome for professional-services
deliverables. This is cheap to test and is the highest-leverage single change available.

## 6. Offer architecture — the core pivot

**The central error: $97 is being asked to be the lead magnet, the profit center, and the whole
relationship simultaneously.** Split those jobs.

| Tier | Offer | Price | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | **Family Payroll Risk Scorecard** — 6 questions, personalized exposure score, what's missing, next step. **Email required.** | Free | Lead magnet. Captures Segment A on fear, everyone else on curiosity. Creates the list. Fires the `Lead` event Meta needs to optimize. |
| **1** | **The Paprika Kit** — CPA-calculated defensible wage, signed opinion letter, templates, labor-law checks, personal video, email support, 1 audit hour | **$297** (test vs. $97 / $497) | Profit center + qualifier |
| **2** | **Done-With-You** — Kit + 30-min CPA session + payroll setup walkthrough + first-year check-in | **$997** | Segment C. Where the margin is. |
| **3** | **Annual Re-Run** — updated wage, refreshed opinion letter, new-year rules | **$147/yr** | Recurring. Multiplies LTV. Legitimate — wages change, kids age, rules change. |
| **4** | **Advisory retainer** — the actual firm relationship | $1,500–3,000/yr | Real LTV; only for fits |
| **W** | **Advisor wholesale** — per-client pricing or seat license + co-branded workpapers | TBD | Distribution |

**Why a risk *scorecard* rather than a savings calculator for Tier 0:** the savings estimator must stay
external for regulatory reasons, so we cannot gate it. A scorecard is a different instrument we can own
and gate, it speaks to the highest-intent segment (A) in the language of fear rather than hope, and its
output is a natural bridge to the paid tier ("your exposure score is 8/10 — here's what closes it").

### The risk-reversal gap

The client has declined a money-back guarantee. That decision has a **quantifiable cost**: for an
unfamiliar seller at $297+, risk reversal is the single cheapest trust device available, and its absence
will depress conversion on a purchase that is entirely trust-dependent. Alternatives that are not a
refund promise:

1. **Pre-qualification promise** — "If a compliant approach won't save you money, we'll tell you before
   you pay." (This is already true; it just isn't offered as a promise.)
2. **Free scorecard as the risk reversal** — prove value before any payment.
3. **Credit-forward** — the $97/$297 credits fully toward the consult, so no money is ever "wasted."
4. **Deliverable-contingent** — payment on delivery of the signed letter rather than up front.

**Recommend presenting options 1 and 3 to Dave as a substitute**, since they cost him nothing and
require no refund exposure.

## 7. Channel strategy

### Meta — reframed from "sales channel" to "attention engine"

The data proves Meta can buy **attention** here astonishingly cheaply: **$6.57–12.70 CPM** and
sub-$0.25 real cost per landing page view. That is a genuine asset. What the data also proves is that
Meta **cannot sell a $97 considered purchase on first touch to a cold audience.** Stop asking it to.

New job for Meta:
- **Top of funnel:** story creative (beard tax, allowance-vs-payroll) → **free scorecard** → email
- **Optimize for `Lead`**, not clicks, not Purchase (insufficient volume to learn on Purchase for months)
- **Age 30–54**, Audience Network **off**, Facebook/Instagram feed + Stories + Reels only
- **Retargeting is mandatory, not optional:** 50%+ video viewers, 30-day site visitors, scorecard
  starters who didn't finish, estimator clickers, checkout abandoners. **A considered purchase converts
  on touch 3–5.** Its total absence to date may account for more lost revenue than any other single gap.
- Creative discipline: **30–45s cuts** (current 127s film loses 90% before the pitch; 12s average watch),
  offer stated in the first 15 seconds on at least one variant, and **qualification written into the copy**
  ("If you own a business and have kids 10–17…") so self-selection assists the algorithm.

### Search — the highest-conversion consumer channel, currently absent

Buyers pre-sold by podcasts are *searching*. Nothing is capturing them.
- Google Search: "hire my kids taxes," "put my child on payroll," "paying kids from business legal,"
  "kids on payroll audit risk," "child W-2 family business"
- Cluster around the **compliance long tail**, which the existing blog already half-covers: workers'
  comp by state, W-2 vs 1099 for a child, entity-type treatment, when a dependent must file
- YouTube search for the same intents (the explainer video already exists)
- **Expect materially better conversion than Meta at higher CPC** — the right trade for a considered purchase

### Advisor wholesale — the compounding engine

Structurally the best economics in the business: one activated advisor delivers many clients at zero
marginal acquisition cost, repeats annually, and Dave has genuine peer credibility (CPA firm,
ReasonableCompensation history, Journal of Accountancy).

Motion: **CPE-accredited webinar** ("Defensible Wages for Family Employees") → sample workpapers →
partner agreement → co-branded delivery. CPE is the correct entry point because it is what this
audience already seeks out and it establishes standing immediately.

**Before another dollar goes in:** of the 66 advisor leads acquired at $27.93, **how many activated,
and how many client files resulted?** If unknown, cap the channel at ≤25% of budget until it is
instrumented. Measure **cost per activated advisor** and **client files per advisor**, never CPL.

### Partnerships — zero-CAC distribution to test

Payroll providers (Gusto, ADP, Patriot) whose users are the exact ICP; bookkeepers and fractional CFOs;
business coaching and franchise networks; parent-entrepreneur communities. Slow, BD-heavy, but the
prospects arrive pre-qualified.

### Owned assets — currently underbuilt

**Email list is the strategic priority.** Today it does not exist; every non-buyer to date is gone
permanently. A list of qualified business-owner parents is the one asset that makes Q4 pushes and annual
renewals cheap forever. Also: the existing blog is a real SEO seed, and the WordPress migration
(`CLAUDE.md` launch cautions) must preserve those URLs.

## 8. The seasonal imperative — this is time-critical

**The strategy has a real, honest deadline: wages must actually be paid during the tax year.** That
creates legitimate urgency without manufactured scarcity — and it means the calendar, not the funnel,
should dictate spend.

| Window | Posture | Message |
|---|---|---|
| **Now → Sep 30** | **BUILD. Minimal media spend.** | Instrument, launch site + scorecard, grow list, produce creative, recruit advisors |
| **Oct 1 → Dec 31** | **PEAK PUSH — concentrate budget here** | "Payroll has to run before Dec 31. Here's what's still possible this year." |
| **Jan → Apr 15** | Second-best window | "You just wrote that check. Set 2027 up now." Do **not** recruit advisors — they're in filing season |
| **May → Sep** | List building, content, advisor recruitment, testing | Education, aspiration, college funding (Aug–Sep) |

**Implication that reframes the whole engagement:** it is late July. Spending into a broken funnel now
is worse than spending nothing. **The next nine weeks are a build sprint for a Q4 revenue window.**
"We're not making sales in July" is close to expected; failing to be ready by October 1 is the actual
risk.

## 10. Economics and decision gates

Post-fix targets. Stated so they can be checked against reality, not as promises:

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| CPM (proven) | $7–13 |
| Cost per qualified landing page view | $0.35–0.60 |
| Landing page view → scorecard lead | 15–25% |
| **Cost per lead** | **$2–5** |
| Lead → purchase within 30 days (nurture + retargeting) | 3–8% |
| **Blended CAC** | **$40–120** |
| AOV at $297 with ~15% Done-With-You attach | ~$400 |
| Renewal attach (year 2) | 30–40% |
| **Estimated LTV** | **$450–700** |
| **Target LTV:CAC** | **≥ 3:1** |

**Kill criteria — the honest test.** Run Phases 0–2 with **$2,000–3,000 over 60 days** (less than the
advisor campaign alone has already consumed). If, with Purchase tracking live, corrected audience and
placements, a story-congruent page, email capture, and an active retargeting layer, the funnel cannot
produce:
- a **lead under $8**, and
- a **sale under $150**, and
- **any measurable Q4 lift**,

then the constraint is the offer itself, and the correct advice is to reposition to B2B-only or stop.
**That test has not yet been run.** Everything to date was measured against a conversion event that
does not exist.
