# Pivot: Advisor Channel First — Strategic Review

**Date:** 2026-08-06 · **Author:** Leaf9 · **Status:** review of Dave's pivot plan
**Supersedes:** the consumer-first ladder in `marketing-strategy.md` §6–7 (see §7 below)

---

## 1. The plan, as proposed

1. Raise price "considerably" from $97; include a meeting and small follow-ups
2. Aggressively target tax-advisor firms of 10–20 preparers — sell one champion, they sell internally
3. Free first case, then sell packages of credits; make it turnkey and an easy way for them to make money
4. Pilot with ~20 friendly firms
5. Pause other marketing (Meta ads, website rebuild) until the pilot is underway

Pricing referenced in the enablement notes: **$475 single case**, **$349/case ten-pack**,
**$499 FA lane** with a client-safe / no-poach pledge.

## 2. Verdict

**Directionally right — this is the pivot the data supported.** Four of the five moves are sound.
The plan's central flaw is that **leverage exists on the sales side and nowhere in delivery**, and its
most expensive risk is not money but relationships: the pilot spends 20 warm CPA friendships as
working capital.

### What's right

| Move | Why it holds up |
|---|---|
| **Raise price + attach a meeting** | Better reasoned than our own $297 recommendation. $97 undercut the authority the product sells; adding live delivery makes $475 a *coherent professional-services offer* rather than a price hike. Fixes credibility and unit economics in one move. |
| **Advisor channel as primary** | Was our #1 recommendation on economics. One activated advisor delivers many clients at near-zero marginal acquisition cost, and repeats annually. |
| **10–20 preparer firms** | Correct band. Large enough for internal volume, small enough that one champion can drive adoption without procurement, committees, or a national-office sign-off. |
| **Warm pilot of ~20** | Correct method. Holds acquisition constant so the pilot tests the *offer*, not the outreach. Fast and nearly free. |
| **Pause Meta** | Right for now. With no Purchase event ever installed, continued spend produced no learning. Pausing costs nothing we can measure. |

## 3. The four risks

### Risk 1 — Delivery capacity. Severe.

Every case needs Dave's judgment and signature, and now a meeting too. Rough arithmetic:

| | |
|---|---|
| Reachable preparers | 20 firms × 10–20 = **200–400** |
| Free first cases promised | **20 unpaid deliveries** before meaningful revenue |
| Time per case (intake review, wage calc, opinion letter, 30-min call, follow-ups) | **60–90 min of Dave specifically** |
| Free-case load | **20–30 hours before a dollar arrives** |
| If the pilot works (5 cases/firm) | **100 cases ≈ 100–150 hours ≈ 3–4 weeks of nothing else** |

The channel's appeal is leverage; the delivery model has none. And because the pilot runs on friends,
slow delivery costs a *relationship plus its referrals*, not just a sale. That is a far more expensive
failure than a failed ad test.

**Mitigations — decide before launch, not during:**
1. **Set max cases/week** and publish a turnaround SLA Dave can actually hit under load.
2. **Stage the free cases** — 5 firms at a time, not 20. Learn on cohort one.
3. **Unbundle Dave's time.** What genuinely requires the CPA is the *judgment and the signature*.
   Intake QC, BLS wage pull, document assembly, and scheduling are not that — template and delegate
   them. This is also the only path to scaling the channel later.
4. **The meeting is the biggest sink.** Consider a group onboarding webinar for advisors plus the
   async personal video the product already includes for clients, reserving live 1:1 time for
   ten-pack tiers or as a paid upsell.

### Risk 2 — "Free first case" selects for the wrong case

Free attracts the messy edge case the advisor is unsure about: maximum time, worst first impression,
weakest proof. It also produces exactly the failure already identified — *"signed up but didn't act."*

**Fixes:**
- Gate the free case behind a **20-minute onboarding call** (advisor invests time = commitment; Dave
  qualifies the case before it consumes a slot).
- **30-day expiry** on the free case to force action.
- Publish **good-fit screening criteria** and ask for a matching case: sole prop or S-corp, kids
  10–17, owner income $150k+. A clean first delivery is what sells the ten-pack.

### Risk 3 — The pilot must produce transferable proof, not just revenue

Warm friends are not cold firms. Scaling assumes results transfer, and nothing in the plan captures
the evidence that would let it. Instrument for:

- Per-firm **activation rate** and **time-to-first-case**
- **Ten-pack conversion** rate
- **2–3 written advisor case studies stated in the advisor's own economics** — "I billed $1,200, it
  cost me $349, it took 20 minutes of my time." This is the single most valuable output of the pilot,
  and cold outreach in month 3 has nothing to lead with without it.

### Risk 4 — The website is pilot infrastructure, not "other marketing"

Every advisor pitched will Google PaprikaTax before putting client relationships behind it. Today
they find a **consumer site selling a $97 kit** — which contradicts a $475 professional pitch and
reads as an info-product shop.

**Correction to the plan:** pause the *consumer* rebuild, but the **advisor-facing surface and price
coherence now matter more, not less**. Concretely:

- **Price coherence is urgent.** If cases become $475, every live surface saying $97 becomes a
  liability *during the pilot*: the WordPress site, the checkout, and the pages we just built. An
  advisor who sees $97 publicly will not sell a $475 case.
- The `/tax-advisors` page is now the most important page on the site. It exists and is built.

## 4. What's missing

1. **The advisor's economics are the entire pitch — and they're unstated.** "Easy way to make money"
   has to be numbers. Framework below; **Dave must confirm what his advisors actually bill**:

   | | |
   |---|---|
   | Advisor cost (ten-pack) | $349 |
   | Typical client bill as a planning deliverable | $995–1,500 *(to confirm)* |
   | Advisor's own time | ~20–30 min (handoff, review, present) |
   | **Advisor net per case** | **~$650–1,150** |

   Doing this research in-house would take a preparer 2–4 hours at $200–400/hr — which is precisely
   why almost none of them do it. That contrast is the headline of the one-pager, not our feature list.

2. **The single-vs-ten-pack gap may be too narrow.** $475 → $349 is 26.5% off for a **$3,490 prepaid
   commitment**. Rather than discount harder (which devalues the single case), load the ten-pack with
   non-price value: priority turnaround, co-branded client-facing scorecard, quarterly office hours,
   listed partner status. Increases perceived value *and* switching cost.

3. **Renewal is undesigned.** A ten-pack consumed and re-bought annually is a subscription in
   disguise. Annual re-runs (wages change, kids age, rules change) should be built into the pilot from
   day one — it roughly doubles LTV per firm and is what makes the channel compound.

4. **Relationship ownership needs to be in writing for both lanes.** The FA no-poach pledge is a smart
   instinct; tax advisors need the same clarity. The site copy already promises PaprikaTax
   communicates only through the listed preparer — formalize that as a written term.

5. **The FA lane is a different sale and should not run inside this pilot.** Financial advisors don't
   prepare returns, so they can't submit cases the same way; their motion is *referral or co-branded
   value-add*, not credits. Running two motions through 20 firms muddies the read. Sequence it after
   the tax-advisor lane produces its first case studies.

6. **Pilot success criteria, defined now.** Suggested: of 20 firms — **≥50% submit a first case within
   30 days**, **≥25% buy a ten-pack within 60 days**, **≥2 usable case studies**. Missing these is
   information, not failure.

7. **The consumer work becomes advisor enablement rather than waste.** The Risk Scorecard + 100 Job
   Ideas we just built can be **white-labeled for advisors**: a client-facing tool carrying the
   advisor's logo that opens the conversation and flags which of their clients qualify. That is
   literally the "make it turnkey" requirement, it repurposes finished work into the pilot, and it
   becomes a lead-generation mechanism *inside the advisor's own book*.

## 5. Phase 0 web fixes — verified, and two of three are not what they appear

Checked live on 2026-08-06:

| Item | Finding | Action |
|---|---|---|
| robots.txt on **paprikatax.com** | **Already correct.** `User-agent: * / Disallow:` (allows everything), sitemap declared, both `sitemap_index.xml` and `wp-sitemap.xml` return 200. No AI crawler is blocked. | **Nothing to fix — skip it.** |
| robots.txt on **app.paprikatax.com** | **No robots.txt exists** — the path returns the SPA shell with HTTP 200. | Real issue, but the fix is the **opposite** of "allow crawling" — see below. |
| Estimator on a crawlable domain | **It is not, and cannot be indexed where it lives.** All app pages return an identical 3,417-byte shell with **zero** content keywords in raw HTML, one generic `<title>PaprikaTax</title>`, no canonical, no meta description. | Real work. See below. |

**Why app.paprikatax.com should be blocked, not opened:**

- It is a client-rendered SPA with **no server-rendered content**. AI crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot,
  PerplexityBot) generally do not execute JavaScript, so these pages contribute **nothing** to AI
  visibility no matter what robots.txt says.
- **It is a crawl trap.** `/this-page-does-not-exist-xyz` returns **HTTP 200** with the same shell —
  an unbounded surface of identical thin pages, with no canonical tags to collapse them.
- Correct move: add a real `robots.txt` that disallows crawling of the app, and put indexable content
  on the main domain instead.

**The actual Phase 0 work, reprioritised:**
1. Add a real `robots.txt` to app.paprikatax.com **disallowing** crawl (client-side task).
2. Publish a genuinely indexable **estimator/scorecard page on `paprikatax.com`** — server-rendered,
   with real copy, methodology, and FAQ content. The tool can still hand off to the app; what needs to
   be crawlable is the *content around it*.
3. GSC: confirm indexing and submit the sitemap (Ben — needs console access).

> ⚠️ **Architecture note, disclosed because it affects our own work:** the site we rebuilt is also a
> client-rendered Vite SPA. That is correct for `noindex` ad landing pages, but it has the *same*
> limitation for organic and AI discovery. If content/SEO stays in the plan (Dave's earlier
> "8 content pieces"), we need prerendering or static generation before publishing content there —
> otherwise the articles are invisible to AI crawlers too. Small, well-understood change; better
> decided now than after eight articles ship.

## 7. Effect on the existing strategy doc

`marketing-strategy.md` recommended a consumer-first ladder (free scorecard → $297 kit → $997
done-with-you) with the advisor channel built in parallel. **This pivot inverts that order**: the
advisor channel becomes the primary engine and consumer acquisition pauses.

What survives from that document, unchanged:
- The ICP analysis, and especially **entity type as the value driver** (sole prop / SMLLC with under-18
  children saves income tax *and* self-employment tax — roughly double an S-corp owner's benefit).
  This is the screening criterion for pilot cases.
- The pain map, the loss-aversion finding, and the verified authority narrative (ReasonableCompensation
  2011–2015, *Journal of Accountancy* May 2012) — which lands *harder* with a CPA audience than a
  consumer one. Dave built software for accountants; this pilot sells to accountants.
- Q4 seasonality: wages must be paid inside the tax year. **The pilot has a real deadline** — advisors
  need cases submitted before 31 December, so the useful selling window closes in about 20 weeks.
- Delivery-capacity and regulatory-boundary risks — both now urgent rather than theoretical.
